How it works

Face mode: an on-device face tracker places three regions on your forehead and cheeks, and the POS remote photoplethysmography method separates the pulse from lighting changes. The red glow is your own pulse, amplified so you can see it.

Fingertip mode: the rear camera and flash read the pulse through your fingertip and also give a perfusion index, the strength of blood flow in the finger, which is one of the signals used for anaemia research.

Eyelid mode: the plain white paper tells Naadi what true white looks like under your current light, so the eyelid colour can be corrected before it is measured. Paler inner eyelids are a classic sign of anaemia. This prototype measures calibrated colour; the hackathon build fuses it with the fingertip signal into a risk band.
